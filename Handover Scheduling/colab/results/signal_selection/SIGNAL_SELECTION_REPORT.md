## Q3. Why Learn-Then-Test for calibration?

The calibration step must give a statement of the form *"the selected threshold adds failures to at most 5% of new inputs, with 90% confidence"*, for a threshold chosen from many candidates.

| Framework | What it guarantees | Fit for this problem |
|---|---|---|
| Naive selection (most aggressive threshold with calibration risk ≤ α) | Nothing | ❌ Overfits the calibration split (see the re-split check below) |
| Cross-validation (Appendix D pilot) | No finite-sample guarantee | ❌ With 36 inputs it could not certify α = 5% at all |
| Split conformal prediction | Coverage of prediction sets | ❌ Answers a different question (intervals, not the risk of a chosen threshold) |
| Conformal Risk Control (Angelopoulos et al.) | Risk ≤ α in expectation | ❌ Weaker (average, not 1 − δ confidence) and needs risk monotone in λ |
| RCPS (Bates et al.) | Risk ≤ α with probability 1 − δ | ❌ Needs risk monotone in λ |
| **Learn-Then-Test** (Angelopoulos et al.), exact binomial, fixed sequence | Risk ≤ α with probability 1 − δ for the selected threshold | ✅ No monotonicity needed (Remark 3.2); controls error across many thresholds; exact test for failure counts |

**Sample size needed to certify α = 5% at δ = 10% even with zero failures:** exact binomial n ≥ 45; Hoeffding n ≥ 461. With 200 calibration speakers (or 200 per re-split half), a Hoeffding-based test can never certify any threshold, while the exact binomial test allows up to 5 failures.

### CPU labels (phase 2 / phase 3 grids; original λ̂ = 0.50 calibration)

**Monotonicity (full calibration split, λ grid 0.20–1.20):** robust risk **decreased** at 3 of 20 steps to a larger λ: λ 0.45 → 0.50: 5 → 4 failures; λ 0.55 → 0.60: 6 → 3 failures; λ 0.90 → 0.95: 4 → 3 failures. Risk is not monotone in λ, so the CRC and RCPS assumptions do not hold, while LTT needs no such assumption.

**Re-split check** (200 random 50/50 speaker re-splits of 400 calibration + test speakers; calibrate on one half, check on the other):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive selection | 3.5% | 3.54% | 5.00% | 17.04 | 0.0% | 1.20 |
| Hoeffding fixed-sequence | 0.0% | 0.00% | 0.00% | 21.00 | 100.0% | — |

**Is LTT a contribution?** No: LTT, its binomial p-values and fixed-sequence testing are existing methods (Angelopoulos et al.; Bauer, 1991) and must be cited as such. The contribution is the adaptation: casting the teacher-to-student handover point as a risk-controlled selection problem, defining a reference-relative added-failure risk (Definition 3.1), building a data design that meets LTT's independence assumption (speaker-disjoint splits, one utterance per speaker), and showing the guarantee holds on held-out speakers. CALM (Schuster et al., 2022) made the same kind of contribution for early exit in language models.
