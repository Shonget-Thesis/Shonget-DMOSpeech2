Slide 1 of 5 • The Research Problem

The Fixed Handover Dilemma in Fast TTS

Motivation
Core Dilemma: In modern two-stage non-autoregressive speech synthesis (like DMOSpeech 2), a deep Flow-Matching Teacher establishes phonetic alignment, then hands off to a fast Consistency Student to finish synthesis. Where should this handover occur?
⚠ Fixed Early Handover (Step 4 / 13 NFE)

Pros: Cuts inference latency by 38.1% (saves 8 model evaluations).
Catastrophic Failure: On noisy or phonetically complex prompts, early handover fails 64.0% of speakers, causing severe acoustic collapse, word dropouts, and robotic garble.

⌛ Fixed Conservative Handover (Step 8 / 21 NFE)

Pros: High synthesis fidelity and robust against noise (0% failures).
Wasteful Compute: Runs 21 full forward evaluations on clean, simple audio prompts that could have cleanly completed at Step 4, wasting valuable CPU resources.

Thesis Objective: Can we design an input-adaptive handover policy that automatically exits early on clean speech to maximize savings, while guaranteeing reliability under PAC statistical risk bounds?

Slide 3 of 5 • Mathematical Formulation

Dual-Mechanism Policy & PAC Risk Control

Formulation
🛡 Mechanism 1: Prompt SNR Safety Gate
SNR = 10 · log10( P_speech / P_noise ) ≥ 20.0 dB

If prompt noise is high ($\text{SNR} < 20.0\text{ dB}$), early exit is immediately prohibited and synthesis is routed to Step 8. This eliminates noise-induced collapse.

📈 Mechanism 2: Zero-NFE Tangent Drift Probe
d_s = || v_s - v_{s-1} ||2 / || v_s ||2 ≤ λ̂ = 0.50

Probes trajectory curvature during teacher ODE integration. If drift $d_s \le 0.50$, the manifold is stable and early handover is safely executed.

⚗ Learn-Then-Test (LTT) Statistical Risk Bound
P( R(λ̂) > α ) ≤ δ • (α = 0.05, δ = 0.10, p = 0.0264)

Calibrated across 200 distinct calibration speakers using fixed-sequence testing to bound the Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER). Guarantees that the expected failure rate will not exceed 5% with 90% confidence.
