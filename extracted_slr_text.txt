Prepared for: Mark Vincent D. Limpahan and the undergraduate thesis team
Search cutoff: 19 September 2026, UTC
Manuscript status: Adviser-facing research working paper; not peer reviewed
Review designation: Structured evidence review with a prospective search protocol and
targeted full-text screening; not an exhaustive systematic literature review
Scope: Inference formulas. Duration prediction and cadence calibration are held outside the
proposed intervention.
Decision: Retain endpoint relaxation as the principal research formulation. Compare
a fixed acceptance weight with an adaptive displacement cap at the first student
reconstruction. Treat cosine weighting as an ablation, not an established solution.
The evidence does not justify declaring any candidate the empirically optimal “final
formula.”
Abstract
Background. The project initially framed teacher–student handover as an abrupt replace-
ment of continuous velocity fields. Source inspection shows a more specific implementation:
partial teacher integration, extrapolation to an endpoint estimate, and stochastic student re-
noising/reconstruction. The correct mathematical object therefore materially affects the proposed
contribution.
Objective. To assess relevant formulas, distinguish evidence of numerical or distributional
mismatch from evidence of audible artifacts, and select a defensible formulation for investigation
under frozen-model constraints.
Methods. A protocol was recorded before external searches. Twenty-eight web-search queries,
targeted primary-source retrieval, reference following, and limited forward searches were used.
Seventeen technical works were retained for targeted full-text assessment, alongside PRISMA
reporting guidance and three project sources.
Extraction covered equations, assumptions,
computational requirements, evidence directness, and transfer limitations. Ranked web results
were not treated as exhaustive database exports; no meta-analysis or independent duplicate
screening was performed.
Results. The literature supports investigating inference parameterization, input-distribution
compatibility, and computation allocation. It does not establish that this project’s handover
causes audible noise. Continuous-field interpolation requires shared-state compatibility and can
add network evaluations. Smooth weights do not establish distribution preservation or perceptual
improvement. Existing relaxation theory supplies a mathematical precedent for partial updates,
but its convergence assumptions are not verified for the stochastic student chain.
Recommendation. Investigate first-handover endpoint correction, with the adaptive candidate
E = T + λ(S −T), where λ = min{1, ρ/ RMS(S −T)} for a nonzero correction and λ = 1
otherwise. This is a projection onto a ball centered on the teacher endpoint, not a new general
projection theorem. It adds no neural evaluations and guarantees a prescribed local displacement
bound. Whether that restriction improves speech, preserves diversity, or merely suppresses useful
student corrections must be tested.
Keywords: text-to-speech; flow matching; distillation; endpoint relaxation; teacher-guided
sampling; inference; numerical analysis.

1. Introduction and bounded problem statement
The thesis needs a problem whose existence can be investigated and a formula whose consequences
can be defended. An attractive interpolation curve is insufficient: the quantities being combined
must mean the same thing, and the intervention must address a measured limitation.
The motivating task is to preserve useful teacher information while allowing an efficient student
to refine a speech representation. Three distinct questions arise:
1. Is the teacher-to-student input compatible with the student’s learned reconstruction behav-
ior?
2. Is accepting the entire first student correction beneficial across inputs?
3. Can an inexpensive acceptance rule improve the quality–cost trade-off?
These questions allow a negative answer. A successful baseline may need no handover modification.
A large correction may fix a bad teacher estimate. A smooth change in latent space may erase
phonetic detail.
The bounded problem statement is therefore:
In a frozen teacher-assisted TTS reconstruction chain, the relationship between the
magnitude and direction of the first student endpoint correction and final synthesis
quality has not been established by the inspected project evidence. This study should
characterize that relationship and test whether bounded acceptance offers a useful
quality–efficiency trade-off.
“Not established by the inspected evidence” is intentionally narrower than “never studied.” This
review cannot certify worldwide novelty.

3. Review methodology
3.1 Protocol and scope
The protocol was recorded before external literature searching, after inspecting the current
project specification and adviser report. The main date window was 2020 through 19 September
2026; earlier mathematical foundations were eligible. English-language accessible full texts
were prioritized. Preprints were eligible but were distinguished from confirmed proceedings
publications.
5


==================== PAGE 6 ====================

The review included: the exact baseline; flow and diffusion parameterizations; accelerated
sampling; teacher/student reconstruction; relaxation; switched-system stability; and closely
related adaptive guidance. It excluded unrelated uses of “teacher,” “handover,” and “consistency,”
secondary AI summaries as evidence, and mathematical conclusions available only from abstracts.
The extraction fields were fixed in advance: problem, original equation and location, normalized
interpretation, assumptions, mathematical property, experimental support, computation, and
transfer limitations. The synthesis was organized by mechanism rather than publication count.
3.2 Sources and search procedure
Discovery used a web search service, not authenticated Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore,
or ACM Digital Library database exports. Primary texts were accessed through arXiv, ACL
Anthology, PMLR, an author-hosted control paper, and official reporting guidance. Domain
filters and title-specific searches were attempted; several queries returned substantial irrelevant
material or publisher-root URLs. This limits sensitivity and reproducibility.
Appendix A contains the 28 exact submitted search strings. Search results led to full-text retrieval.
The DMOSpeech 2 and DMD2 reference lists also directed attention to flow matching, consistency,
and numerical-sampling work. Targeted forward searches used DMOSpeech 2, blending, handover,
and trust-region terms. They are not a complete forward-citation census.
Versions were consolidated by title and arXiv identifier. Venue-search snippets were not accepted
as definitive publication metadata when the actual proceedings record could not be resolved.
The source version examined is distinguished from the possible later publication status.

3.4 Appraisal and synthesis
Each work was assessed qualitatively for directness, mathematical transparency, empirical
relevance, computation reporting, and applicability to frozen checkpoints. No pooled quality
score was used: a correct theorem under unsuitable assumptions is not strong evidence for speech
improvement.
No effect-size meta-analysis was appropriate because image FID, TTS WER, subjective ratings,
diversity measures, and fixed-point residuals are different outcomes. Published performance
numbers were not pooled or treated as comparable across hardware.
PRISMA 2020 is a reporting guideline with a 27-item checklist; using its principles does not
make an incomplete search exhaustive. This review reports its limitations accordingly. PRISMA

3.5 Deviations and limitations of the review process
There was one AI-assisted reviewer, no independent screening, no registered protocol, no librarian-
reviewed search strategy, and no author contact. Adaptive searches added recent trust-region
papers after that candidate emerged. Two recent candidates, dots.tts and the on-policy CFG
study, were promoted from preliminary screening to full-text inclusion during the final coverage
check. This is documented exploration, not a preregistered hypothesis test. The work is a
substantive evidence review and analytical manuscript, but it must not be submitted as a
completed exhaustive SLR without extending these procedures.

4.5 Paper versus implementation
The DMOSpeech 2 paper’s Algorithm 2 expresses teacher state handoff followed by endpoint
prediction and re-noising; Appendix B.4 likewise describes endpoint-style student generation. Its
motivation is recovery of diversity lost under distillation, not a demonstrated audible discontinuity
caused by handover. R1
P3 is more specific: it extrapolates a teacher endpoint and re-noises before the first retained
student call. The paper’s notation and the wrapper’s operations are therefore not interchangeable.
The published Algorithm 3 also draws an ϵ but writes z in its re-noising line; P3 unambiguously
draws fresh noise. Reproduction should follow the chosen, versioned implementation and disclose
the discrepancy rather than silently repair the paper.

4.6 What the prior preliminary results establish
P2 reports a 0.85 first-correction weight, conflicting peak-flux values, and different teacher
endpoint norms before the intervention across compared runs. Those observations do not isolate
a causal handover effect. A 15% reduction in the accepted displacement follows algebraically from
that weight; improved naturalness does not. These internal results are motivation for replication,
not an additional independent literature study.

9. Recommended formulation and derivation
9.1 Recommendation and degree of commitment
The evidence justifies choosing an operator-level research family more strongly than a unique
weighting function. Begin with the first-handover relaxation already suggested by P2. Compare
a fixed weight against an adaptive cap. Do not make cosine the thesis’s required centerpiece
before this comparison.
The adaptive formulation below is selected for its interpretability, alignment with P3, exact
geometric property, and absence of extra neural evaluations. Its selection is a reasoned design
recommendation, not a literature-established ranking of synthesis quality.

9.8 Failure modes and rejection conditions
Reject or narrow the method if the teacher endpoint is poor, the student needs a large corrective
update, norm magnitude does not identify harmful cases, or the cap systematically reduces
intelligibility or diversity. Further risks include mixing semantically misaligned endpoints, scale
drift, padding contamination, normalization-dependent thresholds, and downstream amplification.
The projection itself has a derivative kink at its activation boundary; the recommendation
prioritizes an exact displacement limit, not a C1 claim.

11.6 Statistical analysis and decision rule
Choose one primary final comparison: adaptive cap versus the strongest fixed-weight development
winner, with B1 retained as a baseline. Human evaluation should randomize order and conceal
method labels. Treat utterances and speakers as clustered observations; multiple seeds and
ratings are not automatically independent samples. Use paired estimates and a hierarchical or
cluster bootstrap, with uncertainty reported for quality differences and timing ratios. Correct
exploratory multiple comparisons or clearly label them exploratory.
Before final evaluation, specify intelligibility and speaker-similarity non-inferiority margins in
meaningful units; select them with adviser input and pilot variability rather than inventing
universal tolerances. Likewise specify the minimum worthwhile perceptual effect and acceptable
runtime overhead.
Retain the adaptive method only if it improves the prespecified primary outcome relative to B1
and offers a defensible advantage over fixed relaxation, while satisfying secondary constraints. If
it only bounds displacement, report exactly that. If the fixed weight performs as well, prefer the
simpler fixed method. If neither helps, retain full acceptance and revise the claimed mechanism.

12. Theoretical contribution and novelty assessment
The following are already known mathematical ingredients: convex interpolation, cosine easing,
polynomial boundary matching, relaxed iteration, and projection onto a Euclidean ball. Their
appearance in an equation is not sufficient novelty.
A defensible contribution could combine:
1. Correct operator characterization: distinguish teacher endpoint extrapolation from
stochastic student reconstruction, with reproducible instrumentation.
2. Problem evidence: establish when the first correction is beneficial or harmful under
controlled interventions.
3. A justified acceptance policy: select a computable rule from diagnostics, derive its
local property, and delimit its assumptions.
4. Cost-aware validation: demonstrate a reproducible quality benefit at unchanged neural-
evaluation count and report actual overhead.
5. A useful negative finding: show that smooth schedules are unidentifiable on the chosen
grid, or that correction magnitude is an inadequate reliability signal.
For a theoretical thesis, a local projection bound alone is elementary. A stronger analytical
contribution would require an original, relevant condition relating reconstruction mismatch to
downstream error, a justified data-dependent bound, or a consistency/error result for the actual
stochastic operators. Such a result must not assume the desired conclusion or substitute a teacher
endpoint for ground truth without justification.
Provisional contribution statement:
This study characterizes teacher-assisted reconstruction in distilled TTS and eval-
uates bounded acceptance of the first student endpoint correction. It derives local
displacement and conditional sensitivity properties and tests whether these controls
improve perceptual quality at unchanged neural-evaluation count.
This statement is a research objective until experiments are complete. The term “trajectory-aware”
should be retained only if the method actually uses trajectory information; a single residual
norm is more accurately described as displacement-aware or residual-bounded. No final title is
required to commit to cosine.
13. Threats to validity and conclusion
The search was limited to accessible web discovery and selected primary full texts. It was not
exhaustive, and later or differently indexed prior art may change the novelty assessment. Several
works were inspected as preprints; their equations and reported results were not independently
reproduced. The review’s own derivations are conditional and have not undergone external
mathematical review.
The project source snapshot may differ from the checkpoint’s original training and published
implementation. The latest project report contains preliminary confounds. No listening study
or checkpoint-based experiment was performed here. The recommendation’s norm operates in
normalized mel coordinates, where geometric distance is not perceptual distance. The student
can improve on the teacher, so excessive anchoring may be counterproductive.
Conclusion. The best-supported next formulation is an endpoint acceptance operator aligned
with the actual reconstruction chain. The bounded first-correction rule in Eq. (28) is a concrete
candidate with an exact local geometric guarantee and zero additional neural evaluations. It
is not a proven improvement in speech quality or a novel general interpolation theorem. Fixed

Adviser-facing summary
What the literature establishes. Generation formulas must match the learned prediction
type and inference-state distribution. Sampling placement, training/inference mismatch, and
early-stage preservation are credible research concerns. Existing work already includes relaxation,
adaptive guidance, and learned one-step maps.
What remains hypothetical. We have not established that the current handover creates
audible noise or that smaller first corrections improve speech. A cosine curve does not resolve
those questions by itself.
