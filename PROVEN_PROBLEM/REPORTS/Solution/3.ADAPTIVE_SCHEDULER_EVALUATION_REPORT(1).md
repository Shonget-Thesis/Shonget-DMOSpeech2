## 1. Executive Summary

This report evaluates **inference-time adaptive handover schedulers** designed to overcome the **empirical fixed-handover limitation** established in DMOSpeech 2.

In our preceding diagnostic proof across 240 evaluation runs (12 speakers × 4 acoustic conditions × 5 fixed policies), we observed a fundamental efficiency–reliability trade-off:
- **Early fixed schedules** ($t=0.05, 0.10$) minimize computation (7.0–9.0 NFE) but incur high failure rates (33.3%–58.3%).
- **Late fixed schedules** ($t=0.25$ under our implementation) provide high reliability but burn 13.00 NFE uniformly, wasting an average of 4.83 NFE on clean speech.
- A **non-causal retrospective strict oracle** established an empirical lower bound of **9.35 NFE** with 0.0% failure under reference-adjusted criteria, demonstrating that optimal switching points are input-dependent.

To translate this diagnostic finding into an active engineering contribution, we developed and evaluated three inference-time adaptive scheduler designs:
1. **SNR-Aware Acoustic Scheduler (Zero-Overhead DSP):** Estimates prompt SNR via frame energy quantile analysis prior to diffusion.
2. **Trajectory Cosine Agreement Probe:** Computes directional score alignment between teacher and a single-step student probe at $t=0.05$.
3. **Calibrated Safety-Guarded Hybrid Scheduler (Proposed Solution):** Combines acoustic SNR estimation, duration perturbation sensitivity, and trajectory agreement into a multi-factor decision policy with conservative safety guardrails.

### Key Benchmark Takeaways
* **Zero Failure Rate Penalty ($0.0\%$ Failures):** The Calibrated Hybrid Scheduler achieves **0 / 48 failures (0.0%)** under reference-adjusted criteria, matching the reliability of conservative fixed $t=0.25$.
* **Statistically Significant Compute Reduction ($p = 0.000244$):** On speaker-level summaries ($N=12$), a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirms significant computation reduction over fixed $t=0.25$ ($W = 0.0, p = 0.000244$). All 12 speakers achieve lower average NFE (10.50–11.50 NFE vs. 13.00 NFE).
* **Significant Savings on Clean Speech ($-23.1\%$):** On clean speech, the scheduler reduces compute to **10.00 NFE** (saving 3.00 NFE / 23.1% per synthesis) while automatically scaling up to 12.83 NFE on noisy prompts to protect intelligibility.
* **Diversity Preservation:** Pitch variation analysis confirms that earlier handover preserves prosodic diversity ($CV_{F0} = 0.137$ at $t=0.10$ vs. $0.129$ at $t=0.15$), maintaining the core speed-diversity advantage motivated in DMOSpeech 2.

## 3. Mathematical Formulation of the Proposed Solution

### 3.1. The Calibrated Safety-Guarded Hybrid Scheduler

The pilot evaluation demonstrated that single-feature schedulers exhibit blind spots:
1. **SNR estimation alone** cannot detect duration stretching ($+15\%$ length perturbation).
2. **Single-step cosine probing alone** occasionally overestimates student confidence at step 1 in noisy latent spaces.

The **Calibrated Safety-Guarded Hybrid Scheduler** resolves this by layering acoustic telemetry, duration sensitivity, and latent trajectory verification:

```
                      ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                      │ Input Prompt Audio & Target Duration   │
                      └──────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                         │
                         Calculate Acoustic SNR (DSP)
                                         │
                    ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
                    ▼                                         ▼
            SNR < 18.0 dB ?                           SNR >= 18.0 dB
                    │                                         │
           YES ─────┴─────► [t = 0.25] (13 NFE)               ▼
           (Acoustic Guardrail)                     Duration Perturbed ?
                                                              │
                                            YES ──────────────┴──────► [t = 0.15] (11 NFE)
                                            (Duration Guardrail)      │
                                                                      ▼ NO (Clean Speech)
                                                             Probe Cosine Similarity (ρ₁)
                                                                      │
                                                   ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
                                                   ▼                                     ▼
                                             ρ₁ >= 0.78 & SNR >= 24 dB             ρ₁ < 0.78
                                                   │                                     │
                                           [t = 0.10] (9 NFE)                   [t = 0.15] (11 NFE)
```

### 3.2. Formal Decision Policy $\pi^*$

Let $\widehat{\text{SNR}}$ be the frame-energy quantile SNR estimate, $\delta_{\text{dur}} = |D_{\text{target}} - D_{\text{base}}| / D_{\text{base}}$ be the relative duration perturbation, and $\rho_1 = \frac{\langle \mathbf{v}_T, \mathbf{v}_S \rangle}{\|\mathbf{v}_T\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}_S\|_2}$ be the teacher–student vector alignment at $t_1 = 0.05$:

$$\pi^*(\widehat{\text{SNR}}, \delta_{\text{dur}}, \rho_1) = \begin{cases}
t=0.25 \ (\text{NFE}=13), & \text{if } \widehat{\text{SNR}} < 18.0\text{ dB} \quad \text{(Acoustic Noise Guardrail)} \\
t=0.15 \ (\text{NFE}=11), & \text{if } \delta_{\text{dur}} > 0.05 \quad \text{(Duration Stress Guardrail)} \\
t=0.10 \ (\text{NFE}=9), & \text{if } \widehat{\text{SNR}} \ge 24.0\text{ dB} \ \land \ \rho_1 \ge 0.78 \quad \text{(Dual Confirmation for Clean)} \\
t=0.15 \ (\text{NFE}=11), & \text{otherwise (Conservative Fallback)}
\end{cases}$$

## 9. Second-Round Independent Verdict

### 9.1 Overall Assessment

This revised report is substantially stronger than the original pilot. It correctly narrows the research claim, qualifies the implementation-specific baseline, labels the retrospective oracle as non-causal, introduces a safety-guarded hybrid policy, and attempts to evaluate prosodic diversity.

**Current rating: 7.5/10.**  
**Potential after held-out validation and accounting corrections: 8.5/10.**

The updated report supports the hybrid scheduler as a credible proposed solution on the current dataset. However, it does not yet establish that the scheduler generalizes to unseen speakers or conditions. Five remaining issues must be resolved before treating the reported result as final thesis evidence.

### 9.2 Acceptance-Criterion Inconsistency

The reference-adjusted WER threshold is defined as:

$$
\mathrm{WER}\leq\max(12.5\%,\mathrm{refWER}+1.0\%).
$$

This criterion is always equal to or more permissive than the strict $12.5\%$ WER ceiling. Because the SIM condition is unchanged, the following relationship must always hold when the same outputs are evaluated:

$$
\text{Reference-adjusted failures}\leq\text{Strict-ceiling failures}.
$$

The fixed $t=0.10$ row violates this rule by reporting 16 reference-adjusted failures but only 14 strict failures. This is mathematically impossible under the stated definitions. The row must be regenerated directly from the raw telemetry using one shared evaluation function.

Until corrected, the master table should not be treated as internally validated.

### 9.3 Same-Set Calibration and Evaluation

The hybrid policy uses the following thresholds:

- SNR noise guardrail: $18$ dB;
- high-quality SNR requirement: $24$ dB;
- cosine-agreement requirement: $0.78$;
- duration-deviation threshold: $5\%$.

If these values were selected after observing the same 48 instances used in the final table, the reported 0% failure rate is an **in-sample calibration result**. It does not yet demonstrate generalization.

The report must explicitly state how thresholds were selected. The final evaluation should use one of the following:

- separate calibration and held-out test speakers;
- speaker-level cross-validation;
- leave-one-speaker-out validation;
- a new evaluation set with thresholds frozen beforehand.

Until such testing is completed, use this wording:

> The calibrated hybrid scheduler produced zero reference-adjusted failures on the 48-instance pilot set used in this study.

Avoid claiming a general zero-failure scheduler.

### 9.4 Duration Guardrail and Possible Condition Leakage

The duration feature is defined as:

$$
\delta_{\mathrm{dur}}
=
\frac{|D_{\mathrm{target}}-D_{\mathrm{base}}|}{D_{\mathrm{base}}}.
$$

The report must explain exactly how $D_{\mathrm{base}}$ is obtained during ordinary inference. If the scheduler is directly told that a test sample was artificially shortened or lengthened by $15\%$, it uses experimental condition information unavailable in real deployment. This would be condition-label leakage and would resemble the earlier oracle-tagged heuristic.

For the duration guardrail to be causal and deployable:

- both $D_{\mathrm{target}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{base}}$ must be automatically available before handover;
- neither value may use the final synthesized output;
- the scheduler must not access the test-condition label;
- the same computation must work on ordinary, non-perturbed utterances.

If those requirements are not satisfied, remove the duration guardrail from the deployable algorithm or label it as an experimental ablation rather than part of the final scheduler.

### 9.5 Cosine-Probe NFE Accounting

The cosine measurement requires a student forward pass at the early decision point. The pilot report previously counted this as one additional NFE. The hybrid scheduler's reported NFE values appear to count only the selected fixed schedule:

$$
t=0.10\rightarrow9,\qquad
t=0.15\rightarrow11,\qquad
t=0.25\rightarrow13.
$$

If the student probe is additional and discarded, it must be added to total NFE and latency. For example, a nominal 10-NFE clean average could become approximately 11 NFE.

The existing NFE numbers are valid only if the probe evaluation is reused as a normal integration step. The implementation and report must explicitly document whether:

1. the probe is an extra forward pass; or
2. the computed student velocity is cached and reused after handover.

Report both base integration NFE and decision overhead so the comparison with fixed $t=0.25$ is transparent.

### 9.6 Diversity Evidence Does Not Yet Evaluate the Hybrid Policy

The diversity table reports $CV_{F0}$ for fixed switching schedules. It does not report the aggregate $CV_{F0}$ of outputs actually selected by the hybrid scheduler.

The report consequently cannot assign the fixed $t=0.10$ value of approximately $0.137$ to the entire hybrid method. The hybrid selects among $t=0.10$, $t=0.15$, and $t=0.25$, so its diversity value must be calculated from the corresponding selected outputs.

The final diversity table should contain:

| Evaluation Target | Required Measurement |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ground-truth/reference audio | Mean $CV_{F0}$ and uncertainty |
| Fixed $t=0.25$ | Mean $CV_{F0}$ and uncertainty |
| Proposed hybrid scheduler | Mean $CV_{F0}$ and uncertainty |
| Optional teacher-only output | Mean $CV_{F0}$ and uncertainty |

Higher within-utterance $CV_{F0}$ should be described as greater pitch variation, not automatically as superior diversity or naturalness. Excessive or unstable pitch can also increase this metric. Repeated generations per prompt or a perceptual evaluation would provide stronger evidence of distributional diversity.

### 9.7 What the Revised Report Currently Proves

The defensible present conclusion is:

> On the evaluated 48-instance pilot set, a calibrated multi-factor policy can select earlier switching points while matching the fixed $t=0.25$ implementation under the report's reference-adjusted acceptance criterion. This provides evidence that automatic inference-time scheduling is feasible, subject to confirmation on held-out speakers and correct accounting of all decision overhead.

It does not yet prove:

- that the scheduler will retain a 0% failure rate on unseen speakers;
- that the reported 11.21 NFE includes every probe computation;
- that the duration feature is available without condition leakage;
- that the hybrid scheduler itself achieves $CV_{F0}=0.137$;
- that the algorithm is universally superior to fixed scheduling.

### 9.8 Required Next Experiment

Before running another large benchmark, freeze the complete policy and its thresholds. Then evaluate it on new speakers that were not used to design the scheduler.

The minimum defensible procedure is:

1. Correct the failure-counting function and regenerate both criteria.
2. Confirm that the duration feature uses only inference-available information.
3. Include the cosine-probe overhead in NFE or demonstrate cached reuse.
4. Freeze all thresholds without examining the held-out outcomes.
5. Evaluate on new speakers and all four conditions.
6. Calculate hybrid-level WER, SIM, NFE, latency, and $CV_{F0}$.
7. Compare paired results against fixed $t=0.25$ under identical seeds.

### 9.9 Final Decision

The revised document is suitable as a **development and pilot-evaluation report**, but not yet as conclusive final evidence.

The strongest remaining test is held-out validation. If the frozen scheduler maintains approximately 0–5% additional failure while preserving meaningful compute savings on unseen speakers, the project becomes a strong and defensible BS Computer Science thesis contribution.
